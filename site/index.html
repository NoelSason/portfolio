About me

I'm a junior at UC Berkeley, Class of 2028, double majoring in Data Science and Molecular & Cell Biology. In data science my emphasis is computational biology. In biology I'm on the developmental genetics track.

I do research in three groups. At Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, I write the statistical analysis for a study of how disease changes the microbes that live in the body. In the Lewis Lab, I'm learning computational protein design. In Berkeley's Computational Approaches to Human Learning lab, I'm making the images in an open-source math tutor readable to screen readers.

Outside research, I build Scope, a set of study tools I started in early 2026: a Chrome extension that searches a student's course materials, and Lectra, an iPad app for reading and marking them up. I also co-lead the South Indian Society at Berkeley, a student organization with more than 200 members.

I want to work on AI for biology, including the evaluations that show when a model, or a published result, doesn't hold up. A lot of my recent work is that kind of checking.

  2. Research Independent, October 2026

    Auditing an imagined-speech EEG benchmark

    A published benchmark asks a model to tell which of 39 words a person is imagining from their brain signals. I tested whether it measures that. A baseline that never looks at the EEG, only at the previous word, scored 18.8%, above the published 13.88%. With whole recording runs held out, real labels beat shuffled labels by only 0.64 points, within noise. One participant, self-published, not peer-reviewed.

    Python, PyTorch, scikit-learn

    Paper and code on GitHub

  3. Research Research affiliate, 2026 to now

    Microbial traits at Berkeley Lab

    Statistical analysis for a study, not yet published, that links more than 900 microbiome studies to a knowledge graph of microbial traits. The question: does disease shift the microbes at a body site toward traits like oxygen tolerance? I resolved all 17 issues from the lead scientist's statistical review and tested all 317 traits with correction for multiple comparisons. Nine of my pull requests are merged.

    Python, DuckDB, pandas, biological ontologies

You read my name. A human cell can't: we have no amino acid for O. O is pyrrolysine, the 22nd amino acid, and only some microbes, like this one, read UAG as pyrrolysine instead of "stop."
